# company: ienergizer (A-37 noida sec 60)
## Description: 
**iEnergizer** is a business process outsourcing (BPO) company, which means other companies pay it to run parts of their operations, mainly customer support and back-office work.

**Who they are**

The company was founded in 2000 and is headquartered in Noida, India. Anil Aggarwal is listed as its founder. The parent, iEnergizer Limited, is a holding company incorporated in Guernsey, with operations in India and one location in the US. Headcount is reported very differently across sources, from about 10,000 to 27,000, depending on the source and date. Sources also disagree on whether it is listed on the London market or private, so check the company's own filings if that matters to you.

**What the company does**

It runs services such as customer lifecycle management, sales and fulfilment, account receivable (debt collection) management, QA and testing, HR outsourcing, investor and shareholder services, back-office operations, technical support, and market research and analytics. Its work covers customer service, collections, chat, claims and investor relations, delivered through customer contact centers and back-office processing centers. It also offers automation (bots and virtual assistants), social media support, and self-service solutions.

**Who their clients are**

Its clients include a number of Fortune 500 companies in healthcare payers and providers, banking, financial services, insurance, gaming, publishing and legal. Its own site also lists automotive, telecom, consumer durables, eCommerce, retail and travel. One third-party profile describes the customer base as investment and commercial banks, financial services firms, healthcare insurers, mortgage houses, utilities, and online gaming and entertainment companies.

I couldn't find any individual client names in public sources. That is common for BPOs, which usually don't disclose clients because of confidentiality agreements.

**What they do for those clients**

They handle customer interactions over voice, chat and email, covering inquiries, technical support, account management and issue resolution, with performance monitoring and quality standards. In practice, a bank might hand over its collections calls, a health insurer its claims processing, and a gaming company its player support. iEnergizer describes itself as an extension of its clients' operations rather than just an outsourcing vendor. Its pitch is high-quality service at lower operating cost.

## Fresher applications: 

For a fresher, iEnergizer mostly hires for customer-facing support roles, and most of the openings I found were in Noida.

**Roles open to freshers**

- **Customer support (voice, non-voice or blended):** One posting for international non-voice customer service says graduation is mandatory for freshers. It lists roughly 21k in hand for graduate freshers, rotational or US shifts, and cab facility both ways.
- **Gaming support (chat process executive):** This is open to 0-2 years of experience and any graduate or postgraduate, with a listed package of about 3.84-5.08 LPA. It also has a 45-day training period, a 3-month probation and no bond. The role is first-line support for players, covering in-game, billing and technical queries by chat, email and phone.
- **Walk-in hiring:** Some postings describe walk-in interviews for customer service executives, with freshers welcome and immediate joiners preferred.

Some other postings require prior BPO experience, so read the eligibility line before applying.

**What the interview is like**

Reviews on Glassdoor rate the difficulty as fairly low, about 2.69 out of 5, with the process averaging around 9 days across roles. A recent reviewer for a customer service role in Noida described cooperative HR, a manager who cross-questions and digs into your answers, and a Versant test. Common stages are a one-on-one interview and a skills test. Versant is a spoken-English assessment, so practice speaking clearly and confidently.

**How to prepare**

- Learn the basics from my earlier answer: it's a BPO serving healthcare, banking, gaming and similar clients. Be ready to say why you want a support role there.
- Practice self-introduction, "why should we hire you", and handling a difficult customer in English.
- Be sure you can handle rotational and night shifts, since many roles run 24x7 for US, UK and Australian clients.
- Apply through the company's official site (ienergizerbpo.com) or a verified job portal. Many of the postings I found are third-party HR posts with WhatsApp numbers and dated salaries, so treat those figures as rough. Don't pay any fee for a job.

## Role: Customer support 
For iEnergizer customer support roles, the process is fairly simple but can be long. Reviews on Glassdoor describe 2 to 4 short rounds. The exact order varies by process, and the reviews are anonymous.

**The usual rounds**

1. **HR round:** They ask you to introduce yourself and talk about your education and background. One candidate also reported being asked why they wanted to join the company. Expect basic questions about customer service too.
2. **Operations (process manager) round:** A recent reviewer said the manager cross-questions and digs into your answers, while HR was cooperative. Freshers may get questions about communication and handling situations. One reviewer said they were given two topics and had to speak for two minutes on one of them, such as online vs offline media or pollution.
3. **Versant and typing tests:** Some candidates report a combined typing and Versant round. Versant checks your spoken English, and some processes add a writing test. Chat roles include a typing speed test. One reviewer said the typing test also checks active audio, with a pass mark above 60%.
4. **Client round and offer:** Some candidates report a client round and a salary discussion after the earlier rounds. After that comes an offer or letter of intent, then a joining date and document verification.

**Questions people were asked**

- Tell me about yourself.
- Why do you want to join this company?
- Where is your hometown, and how often do you go there?
- How did you spend your last day?
- Do you know what customer service involves?
- A short talk on a random topic, for about two minutes.

**What to expect on the day**

The process is largely about checking your confidence and fluency in English. It can take a long time: one reviewer described it as taking the whole day, and walk-in days can be crowded. One candidate said there were too many people at a walk-in and they only went through two rounds before being told to go home and wait for a call. Another reviewer said the company gives jobs to almost everyone, but I'd treat that as one person's impression.

**How to prepare**

- Prepare a 30 to 60 second introduction and practice it aloud until it sounds natural.
- Practice speaking on random topics for two minutes without long pauses. Speaking clearly matters more than using difficult words.
- Practice typing on a free typing tool, and work on your typing speed and accuracy.
- Prepare a simple answer to "how would you handle an angry customer": listen, apologise, find the issue, offer a solution, confirm.
- Carry a few copies of your resume, ID proofs and education documents, and dress neatly.
- Bring patience and expect to wait for several hours.
- Confirm the shift timings and salary in writing before you accept. Some processes are rotational or night shifts.

If you go through a recruiter or consultancy, check that they are genuine and never pay anyone for the job.
